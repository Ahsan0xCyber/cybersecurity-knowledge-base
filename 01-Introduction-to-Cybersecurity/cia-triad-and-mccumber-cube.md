# CIA Triad and McCumber Cube

## 1. CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is a fundamental information security model consisting of three principles: Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.

### 1.1 Confidentiality

**Definition:** Prevents sensitive information from being disclosed to unauthorized users.

- **Purpose:** Keep information secret from unauthorized people.
- **Methods:** Encryption, access control, and strong passwords.
- **Key Term:** Disclosure = Making information known.

**C = Confidentiality → Keep information secret**

### 1.2 Integrity

**Definition:** Protects information from intentional or accidental modification.

- **Methods:** Hash functions and checksums.
- **Purpose:** Maintain the accuracy and completeness of information.

**I = Integrity → Keep information accurate and unchanged**

### 1.3 Availability

**Definition:** Ensures authorized users can access systems and data when needed.

- **Methods:** Maintenance, repairs, updates, and backups.

**A = Availability → Accessible when needed**

## 2. McCumber Cube (1991)

**Definition:** The McCumber Cube is an information security model created by John McCumber in 1991. It helps organizations establish and evaluate information security.

The model combines three dimensions:

- **Security Principles:** CIA
- **Information States:** PST
- **Security Measures:** ATP

### 2.1 Security Principles — CIA

These principles describe the main goals of information security.

- **Confidentiality:** Prevent unauthorized disclosure.
- **Integrity:** Protect information from unauthorized modification.
- **Availability:** Ensure authorized access when needed.

### 2.2 Information States — PST

Describes the states of information that need protection.

#### Processing

Data currently being used or processed by a system.

- **Also called:** Data in Use.
- **Example:** A computer processing information.

**P = Processing → Data being processed**

#### Storage

Data stored in memory or storage devices.

- **Examples:** HDD, SSD, USB drive.
- **Also called:** Data at Rest.

**S = Storage → Stored data**

#### Transmission

Data travelling between information systems or devices.

- **Also called:** Data in Transit.
- **Example:** Data being sent over a network.

**T = Transmission → Data travelling**

### 2.3 Security Measures — ATP

Describes the measures used to protect information.

#### Awareness, Training & Education

Helps users understand security threats and learn how to protect systems.

- **Example:** Phishing awareness training.

**A = Awareness, Training & Education → Security awareness and knowledge**

#### Technology

Hardware and software solutions used to protect information systems.

- **Examples:** Firewalls, encryption, and antivirus software.

**T = Technology → Security tools**

#### Policy & Procedure

Rules and guidelines for implementing information security.

- **Examples:** Incident response plans and security procedures.

**P = Policy & Procedure → Rules and guidelines**

---

## 3. McCumber Cube — Quick Revision

| Dimension | Meaning | Components |
|---|---|---|
| CIA | Security principles | Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability |
| PST | Information states | Processing, Storage, Transmission |
| ATP | Security measures | Awareness, Training & Education; Technology; Policy & Procedure |

### Remember

- **CIA:** What security principles must be maintained?
- **PST:** What state is the information in?
- **ATP:** What security measures can protect the information?

**McCumber Cube = CIA × PST × ATP**

The three dimensions help organizations consider different combinations of security principles, information states, and security measures.

## Cybersecurity Relevance

The McCumber Cube helps identify security requirements for information during processing, storage, and transmission.

### Important Terms

- **Sensitive Information:** Information that requires protection.
- **Disclosure:** Revealing information.
- **Integrity:** Accuracy and completeness of information.
- **Backup:** An additional copy of data kept for recovery.
- **Data at Rest:** Stored data.
- **Data in Use:** Data currently being processed or used.
- **Data in Transit:** Data being transmitted between systems.
