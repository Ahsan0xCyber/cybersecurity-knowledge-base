# Penetration Testing and Risk Management

## 1. Penetration Testing

Penetration testing (PenTest) is an authorized security assessment that simulates attacks to identify and validate security weaknesses.

It may assess systems, networks, applications, configurations, and security processes.

Testing must follow an agreed scope and authorization.

## 2. Common Penetration Testing Activities

### 1. Planning
- Define the scope, objectives, rules, and authorization.
- Gather relevant background information.
- Identify possible areas of risk.

### 2. Reconnaissance and Scanning
- Gather information about the target.
- Identify accessible hosts, ports, and services.
- Look for potential vulnerabilities.

### 3. Gaining Access
- Validate selected vulnerabilities within the approved scope.
- Determine the possible impact of a weakness.

### 4. Post-Exploitation Assessment
- Assess the impact of validated access.
- Determine what systems or data may be affected.
- Avoid unnecessary changes or disruption.

Maintaining persistent access is not required in every penetration test.

### 5. Analysis and Reporting
- Document findings and evidence.
- Explain the potential impact.
- Recommend remediation and improvements.

**Note:** Penetration testing methodologies vary; not every assessment uses exactly the same stages.

## 3. Risk Management

Risk management identifies, assesses, responds to, and monitors risks.

Risk cannot always be eliminated completely. Organizations determine an acceptable level of risk based on potential impact and available resources.

### Risk Management Process

1. **Frame the Risk:** Define the context and objectives.
2. **Assess the Risk:** Estimate likelihood and impact.
3. **Respond to the Risk:** Select an appropriate response.
4. **Review Continuously:** Monitor changes and reassess risk.

### Risk Assessment Methods

- **Qualitative:** Uses categories such as Low, Medium, and High.
- **Quantitative:** Uses numerical estimates, such as financial loss or probability.

### Risk Response Options

- **Avoid:** Stop the activity that creates the risk.
- **Mitigate:** Reduce the likelihood or impact.
- **Transfer:** Shift some financial or operational consequences to another party.
- **Accept:** Formally acknowledge and retain the risk.

## 4. Security Breach Response

After a breach, organizations should:

- Investigate the cause and determine the scope.
- Identify affected systems and information.
- Remove unauthorized access and address persistence.
- Restore systems safely and verify their security.
- Communicate accurately with affected parties as appropriate.
- Apply patches and corrective controls.
- Educate users and document lessons learned.

## 5. Risk and Security Controls

Security controls should be proportionate to the value of the assets, the potential impact, and the organization's risk tolerance.

**Key Point:** Risk management reduces risk; it does not guarantee that all risk will disappear.
