Data Security Breaches

1. What Is a Data Breach?

A data security breach is an incident in which sensitive or personal data is accessed, disclosed, or stolen without authorization.

2. Real-World Security Incidents

Persirai Botnet (2017)

- Targeted over 1,000 models of IP cameras.
- Exploited open ports to install malware.
- Hijacked more than 122,000 cameras.
- Used compromised cameras to launch DDoS attacks without owners' knowledge.

DDoS (Distributed Denial-of-Service): An attack in which multiple compromised devices overwhelm a target with traffic, making its services unavailable.

Equifax Data Breach (2017)

- Announced in September 2017.
- Attackers exploited a known web application vulnerability.
- Sensitive personal information of millions of people was exposed.
- The exposed information could enable identity theft and fraud.

Fake / Unauthorized Websites

Cybercriminals may create websites that resemble legitimate ones to trick users into revealing personal information.

Protection:

- Check whether your information was compromised.
- Be cautious when sharing personal information online.
- Review credit reports where available.
- Report suspicious or unauthorized activity promptly.

3. Consequences of a Security Breach

A security breach can cause serious harm to an organization.

1. Reputational Damage

- Customers may switch to more trustworthy competitors.
- Employees may lose confidence in the organization.
- Rebuilding reputation can take a long time.

2. Vandalism

- Attackers may modify websites or publish false information.
- Unauthorized changes to contact details can damage credibility and may be difficult to detect.

3. Theft

- Sensitive personal data may be stolen.
- Criminals may use it for financial fraud or identity theft.

4. Loss of Revenue

- Attacks may interrupt online business operations.
- Data loss can slow business growth.
- Organizations may face recovery costs, fines, or penalties.

5. Damaged Intellectual Property (IP)

- Attackers may steal confidential documents, trade secrets, or other intellectual property.
- This can reduce an organization's competitive advantage.

Key Point: Not every cyberattack can be completely prevented. Security teams must detect incidents and respond quickly to minimize damage.

4. Data Breach Prevention

Organizations should improve security practices to reduce the risk of data breaches.

- Cybersecurity Training: Train employees to recognize cyberattacks and suspicious activity.
- Two-Factor Authentication (2FA): Add an extra verification step to protect accounts.
- Logging and Continuous Monitoring: Record system activity and monitor for anomalous behavior.
- Salting + Strong Hashing: Store passwords using unique salts and robust password-hashing algorithms.
- Private Network Isolation: Restrict access to cloud resources and sensitive systems instead of exposing them unnecessarily to the public internet.
- Secure VPN: Use an appropriately secured VPN to protect authorized remote access to internal systems.

5. Key Terms

- Data Breach: Unauthorized access to or disclosure of protected data.
- Botnet: A group of compromised devices controlled by an attacker.
- DDoS: An attack that overwhelms a service with traffic from multiple sources.
- Identity Theft: Misusing another person's personal information to impersonate them.
- Intellectual Property (IP): Creations or information with commercial or legal value.
- Credentials: Information, such as a username and password, used to authenticate a user.
- Salting: Adding a unique random value to a password before hashing it.
- Anomalous Behavior: Activity that differs from expected or normal behavior.

6. Quick Revision

- Persirai: IP camera botnet used for DDoS attacks.
- Equifax: A 2017 breach involving an exploited web application vulnerability.
- Major consequences: Reputation damage, vandalism, theft, revenue loss, and intellectual property theft.
- Major defenses: Employee training, 2FA, monitoring, secure password hashing, network isolation, and VPN access.
